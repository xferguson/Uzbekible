Phone screens

Each screen in light and dark. English appears in exactly two places, the clue in the middle and the reveal after an answer (the decided exception); every word on a tile is Uzbek or Turkish, and the tiles are never glossed.

Prototype

Play a card

Eight real pairs. Drag a tile sideways, tap a tile, press an arrow button, or use ← →. The timer, glaze, crack and reveal behave as drawn; three mistakes end the run.

  • A swipe past the halfway line, a tap, an arrow button or a key all choose the same side.
  • After an answer the card holds for 1.4 s on the reveal (tap or Space to skip), then the next card slides in.
  • The timer shrinks as the streak grows: 8 s at the start, 3 s at the tightest (table below).
  • Reduced motion: no slide, shake or bloom; the state change is instant and keeps its shape cue.
Six scripts

Uzbek in two scripts, Turkish always Turkish

Soxta v1 pairs Uzbek against Turkish, so the board uses Uzbek Latin or Cyrillic and Turkish. The same card is shown below in both Uzbek scripts. The extra row tests the tile with Russian, Persian and Arabic words for the later variant (typography only, not content): Persian and Arabic run right to left and keep their own fonts.

Parts

Tile, timer, lives and streak

Every state of the pieces. Each carries a shape cue beside its colour: a gold glaze with a check, cracks with a cross, a dashed outline for "this was the right one", spent timer segments drawn dashed.

Motion

Glaze, crack, time out

The same easing and shared constants as Juft. Each animation has a reduced-motion replacement that keeps the shape cue and drops the movement.

Proposed numbers · tuned later in J2

How the timer tightens

Config, not code. The mockup shows these values; J2 may adjust them after simulation. The timer is twelve glazed segments that drop out one by one.

Fit

Tile sizes and the narrowest phone

Nothing scrolls. The tiles and the clue column share the width; a long word steps its font size down and wraps rather than shrinking the tile. One frame at 360 px shows the tightest case.

Contrast

Colour pairs

No new tokens. These are the pairs the card uses; the numbers are computed from the same formula as contrast.mjs, which now also checks the timer pair.
